01 · The Climate

Riyadh is not a hot Kuala Lumpur.

Both are hot. That is where the similarity ends, and the differences are not small adjustments — they reverse several of his design rules. Two of the three work strongly in our favour.

01

The night is 17 degrees cooler than the day

Riyadh drops from about 44 °C in the afternoon to about 27 °C by dawn. Kuala Lumpur barely moves, maybe 7 degrees.

Why we care: heavy materials can absorb heat all day and release it at night. In the tropics that never works, so Yeang built light. Here, weight becomes useful.

02

The air is bone dry

At 10% humidity the air is desperate to absorb water, and absorbing water cools it. At Kuala Lumpur's 70% the air is nearly full and evaporation does almost nothing.

Why we care: we can cool outdoor air with water alone. This is the single biggest opportunity he never had. See slide 05.

03

Dust, not damp

Shamal dust storms run three to five days at a time, several times a year. There is almost no rain and no humidity.

Why we care: his details fight mould, condensation and driving rain. Ours must fight dust getting in, materials expanding and contracting, and UV. Different problem, different detailing budget.

02 · Keep

Take these unchanged.

The move
What we do with it
  • 01
    Shape the building before you size the machinesOrientation, massing, depth, shading and mass are free. Cooling plant is not, and it is paid for again every year. He always settles the free things first.
    Do thisNo MEP sizing until orientation, floor-plate depth and the shading strategy are fixed. The stronger Riyadh sun means the gap between a badly shaped and a well shaped tower is wider here than in the tropics, so this is worth more to us than it was to him.
  • 02
    Put the lifts and stairs on the east and west facesLow morning and evening sun strikes those faces almost horizontally. A horizontal louvre cannot block it. A solid block of lifts, stairs, plant and lavatories can, and those spaces do not need a view or a steady temperature.
    Do thisCores east and west, occupied space facing north and south. It also forces the long, narrow, north-south plan a desert tower wants anyway. This is our headline move, and it is his own signature.
  • 03
    Keep the planting joined to the groundHis point is that isolated planting is not habitat. Wildlife needs a continuous route. A green terrace on floor 40 with nothing below it is a pot plant with a good view.
    Do thisOne continuous planted route from ground level upward, and plant what actually grows here: acacia, ziziphus, wadi species. A local ecology reconstructed vertically is far more defensible than an imported one.
  • 04
    Decide the water before the greeneryHe treats nature, water, engineering and people as one system, and says the scarcest one should lead. Here that is unarguably water.
    Do thisSet an annual water budget in litres before anyone draws a planted terrace. The budget then tells us how much greenery is honest, rather than the render telling us afterwards.

03 · Reverse

Take these, but reverse them.

These are the dangerous ones. Copied straight from his tropical work they look correct, they photograph well, and they perform badly or fail outright.

What he does in the tropics
What we do instead
  • 01
    Green over the whole façadeIn Singapore rain is free and constant, so planting can cover everything.
    Reverse itPlanting only where there is already shade and a water line: inside the cooled sky courts, at the base, on protected terraces. Rule of thumb: if a plant needs its own irrigation run to survive, it has to earn its place.
  • 02
    Windows open all yearTropical air is warm but usable. Outside air is always a resource.
    Reverse itFor roughly four months, outside air is a heat source, not relief. The façade must be able to shut. Openable roughly October to April, sealed June to September, with the switch designed rather than left to occupants.
  • 03
    Lightweight constructionMass is useless in the tropics because the night never gets cool enough to discharge it.
    Reverse itUse mass. From October to April the cool night genuinely recharges it. In July, when nights stay at 27 °C, mass no longer gives free cooling but still shifts the load off the afternoon peak, which cuts plant size and tariff cost.
  • 04
    Sky courts open to the breezeAn open terrace works in a climate with a dependable breeze and tolerable air.
    Reverse itEnclose them, shade them, and cool them with water. An open terrace in July is unusable; a shaded, evaporatively cooled court is comfortable. He did exactly this himself at K Tower in Kuwait.
  • 05
    Shade with thin screensLouvres and fins, because there is no benefit in weight.
    Reverse itShade and weight together. Deep reveals, thick shaded walls, small protected openings. This is where Najdi courtyard building, not Malaysia, is the reference we should be looking at.
  • 06
    Mashrabiya as a decorative patternUsually applied as cultural surface decoration on Gulf projects.
    Reverse itMake it a working component doing five jobs at once: block sun, cut glare, give privacy, control airflow, and filter dust. Specify it by performance, not by pattern, and it stops being decoration.

04 · Drop

Leave these behind.

  • 01
    Rainwater as a real water sourceAbout 100 mm falls all year, and none of it between June and September. Keep gutters and tanks for flash-flood management, but a rainwater irrigation claim in Riyadh will be laughed at. Irrigation runs on treated wastewater.
  • 02
    Permanently open, breezy façadesDust storms, 44 °C air, and wind pressure at supertall height rule it out. Every opening needs to be closeable and filtered.
  • 03
    Designing around a prevailing breezeAverage wind is a weak 3.9 m/s and there is no reliable sea breeze to plan around, unlike Penang. If we want air to move, we drive it with the stack effect, which a tall building generates for free.
  • 04
    Detailing against dampMould, condensation, timber rot and deep overhangs against driving rain are near-irrelevant here. That detailing effort should be spent on dust sealing, thermal movement and UV instead.

05 · Cooling

Dry air is our cooling machine.

Dry air cools down when water evaporates into it. The drier the air, the more it cools. Riyadh's July air is about as dry as inhabited air gets, which means the climate that makes irrigation expensive also hands us the cheapest cooling there is.

44 °C → 24 °C
What water alone does to July afternoon air, at a good evaporative cooler's performance. No refrigeration, no compressor.
28 °C
Even with a conservative, cheaper system. Still comfortable shade on the worst afternoon of the year.
Near zero
What the same technique achieves in Kuala Lumpur. At 70% humidity the air is already nearly full and cannot take the water.

This is the most important idea in the chapter. It is why the open tropical sky court becomes a shaded, cooled desert court, and it is exactly what Yeang proposed on the north face of his Kuwait tower. It converts our worst climate problem into the thing that makes outdoor space usable in July. Every sky court, podium and terrace on Burj Fitrah should be designed around it.

And it costs water, so budget it. Evaporative cooling consumes what it evaporates. Irrigation and cooling draw on the same budget, which is the argument for treated wastewater supply and a xeric planting palette. Say the number out loud in the proposal rather than leaving it to be discovered.

06 · Carbon

Four things we can actually do about the concrete.

Most of a tower's carbon is emitted before anyone moves in, making the concrete and steel. Energy efficiency does not touch it. These four moves do, and all four have to be started at concept stage or they are lost.

The move
What it is worth, and who does it
  • 01
    Replace part of the cementCement is where nearly all the concrete carbon sits. Substituting a share of it is the single biggest technical lever available to us.
    Worth roughly a quarter to a third of the concrete's carbonThe catch: the two standard substitutes are by-products of coal power and blast furnaces, and Saudi Arabia has little of either, so they would be imported. The local alternative is natural volcanic pozzolan from the harrat fields in the west. Action: ask a Saudi ready-mix supplier to price and test both, now, before the structural scheme is fixed.
  • 02
    Use less material for the same buildingHigher strength concrete, efficient floor systems, a structural grid chosen for material efficiency rather than habit. Height compounds this: every extra floor adds material to everything below it.
    Worth 10 to 20% if it is a target from day oneAction: give the structural engineer a kilograms of concrete per m² target at concept stage, in the same brief as the cost target. Nobody optimises for a number they were never given.
  • 03
    Make it last, and make it changeableThe carbon is spent once. Spread across a building that serves 100 years and several different uses, the annual figure falls. Spread across one that is refitted or replaced in 40, it does not.
    Changes the denominator, not the number, and that is still realAction: generous floor-to-floor heights, serviceable structure, cores and risers sized to be re-planned. Then state a design life in the proposal and hold ourselves to it.
  • 04
    Measure it and publish itNo Gulf tower has ever published a whole-life carbon figure. Not Burj Khalifa, KAPSARC, Msheireb, Masdar or Al Bahar. They hold certificates; none holds a number.
    Worth nothing in carbon, and everything in positionIt also forces the three moves above to actually happen, because you cannot improve what nobody counted. Action: commission the assessment at concept stage, not at completion, so it can still change decisions.

And the honest limit. Do all four well and the concrete still lands near 350 kg of CO₂ per m² — which is the strictest cap in force anywhere for an entire building, and we would have spent it on the structure alone. The only lever big enough to close that gap is building less tall, and that is not ours to pull. If the height is fixed by the brief, we have bought a landmark with carbon. Worth saying to each other now rather than hearing it in a review.

CaveatsPercentages are indicative ranges from our own working, not a published study. Engineers to confirm before any of it reaches a client.Never claim the tower is low carbon, that density justifies the height, or that energy performance answers the objection.

07 · Decisions

What we do on Burj Fitrah.

01

Cores east and west

Lifts, stairs and plant on the two faces taking low sun. Occupied space north and south. Fix this at massing stage, because everything else follows from the plan it produces.

02

Cool the courts with water

Every sky court shaded, enclosable and evaporatively cooled. 44 °C to 24 °C on dry air alone. This is what makes outdoor space work in July, and it is the heart of our climate story.

03

Water budget before greenery

Litres per year agreed first, on treated wastewater, with a xeric palette. The budget decides the planting. Publish it rather than hide it.

04

Build heavy where it pays

Mass and deep shade together. It works for eight months of the year and shifts peak load in the other four. Najdi building is the reference, not Malaysia.

05

Detail for dust

His envelopes corroded in humidity. Ours face dust, thermal movement and UV. Move the detailing effort there, and hold a higher standard than his built work reached.

06

Publish the carbon

Nobody in the Gulf has. Going first turns our most exposed flank into the most defensible thing about the project.
