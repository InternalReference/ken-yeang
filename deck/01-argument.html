01 · The Man

An architect who trained as an ecologist.

Most architects who talk about ecology mean it as a metaphor. Yeang studied it as a science, and joined the scientific society to prove it.

  • 01
    Cambridge, from 1971Began doctoral research on ecological design after the Architectural Association in London. Among the first architects anywhere to take a PhD on the subject.
  • 02
    British Ecological Society, 1975He joined a scientific society, not an architectural one. He also took ecology coursework at Cambridge, and studied ecological land-use planning under Ian McHarg at Pennsylvania.
  • 03
    Kuala Lumpur, mid-1970sHe did not found his practice. He joined a firm started by Tengku Robert Hamzah and became a shareholding partner. Hamzah had studied tropical architecture under Otto Koenigsberger, so the climate science came through both men.
  • 04
    Still in the prototypeHe has lived in the Roof-Roof House, his own 1984 experiment in passive cooling, for forty years. Around 200 built projects since.
Menara MesiniagaSubang Jaya, 1992

Know this before anyone else finds it. His attempt to scale internationally failed. Llewelyn Davies Yeang ran from 2005 and went into liquidation in April 2013, costing around 35 London jobs. Two further UK companies he set up were dissolved in 2014 and 2019. The trade press blamed bad debt and an international pipeline that never arrived, not his conduct. The Kuala Lumpur practice has run continuously for about fifty years. Tengku Robert Hamzah died in 2025.

02 · Ecodesign

Join the building to what was already living there.

Strip away the vocabulary and he has one idea. A site is a living system before we arrive. A building either joins that system or interrupts it. His whole career is about joining.

  • 01
    Design it like a transplant, not an objectHis own image is a prosthesis: a made thing joined to a living body without being rejected by it. That means matching what is already there physically, in how systems flow, and over time as both change.
  • 02
    Nature needs to move, so keep it connectedWildlife lives in patches joined by corridors. Cut the corridor and the patch dies, however green it looks. This, not aesthetics, is the real argument for planting that runs unbroken from the ground upward.
  • 03
    A green building on its own is a dead endIf our planting connects to nothing, it is a garden on a shelf. If it links to the ground, to a park, to a wadi system, it is habitat. The test is connection, not quantity.
Terraced plantingMesiniaga façade

One thing people get backwards. Climate-responsive design sits inside ecological design, not alongside it. Shading and ventilation are one part of a larger argument about the living system. He says this repeatedly, and it is the difference between a comfortable building and an ecological one.

SourcesYeang, Strategies for Green Design, 2009; RIBA Journal, Hindsight interview.His named influences: Ian McHarg, Alfred North Whitehead, Ludwig von Bertalanffy.

03 · Ecomimesis

Copy how an ecosystem works, not how a plant looks.

01

The distinction

Copying a leaf or a termite mound is biomimicry. Copying how a whole ecosystem behaves — its cycles, its flows, who eats what, where the water goes — is what he calls ecomimesis. Same instinct, much bigger scale.

02

What it means in practice

Design the building as a small ecosystem. Water arrives, is used, is cleaned, is used again. Waste from one part feeds another. Nothing leaves that could have stayed. The form follows from the flows.

03

The test to apply

Point at any planted surface on our drawings and ask: what job does this do? Habitat, shade, water, food, cooling. If the answer is "it looks green", it is decoration and it will be the first thing cut.

"Ecological design is still very much in its infancy. The totally green building or green city does not yet exist."

Ken Yeang, 2009

Worth rememberingAn independent reviewer criticised one of his own buildings for planting that was decorative rather than shading. Even he fails this test. See chapter 3.

04 · The Energy Ladder

Spend nothing before you spend something.

This is his most useful contribution and it is not a theory, it is a working order. Most projects begin with the mechanical solution and add greenery afterwards. He begins at the top of this ladder and only moves down when the rung above genuinely runs out. Every step down costs money to install and money forever after.

And in a real building, all of these at once. Yeang's fifth term is simply the honest admission that a tower runs different rungs in different places, on different days. That is not a compromise. It is the point. What we should be able to state for Burj Fitrah: which rung each space sits on, and why.

The line worth stealing. At the Singapore National Library he put the book collections on full air conditioning and said so plainly, calling it "an acknowledgement that comfort is as much a question of lifestyle and perception as it is about cost and energy." He does not pretend passive design goes everywhere. For a Riyadh client who expects full conditioning, that honesty is far more persuasive than purism.

05 · Four Systems, One Design

A building is four systems, not one.

Normally these are four separate consultant packages that meet at coordination meetings and fight over space. His argument is that they are one system, and that whichever is scarcest on your site should drive the design. On our site that is water.

Nature

The living layer. Soil, planting, habitat, the species that were on the site before us and the ones we could bring back.

Ask on our projectWhat lives here now, and does our tower connect it to the ground or cut it off?

Water

Every drop in and out. Supply, irrigation, waste, reuse, and water used deliberately as a cooling device.

Ask on our projectWhat is our annual water budget, where does it come from, and can we say it out loud?

Engineering

Structure, envelope, services, energy. The built and mechanical layer. He deliberately ranks it below the two above.

Ask on our projectWhat is the machinery covering that better shaping could have handled for free?

People

Use, behaviour, regulation, ownership, who is allowed in and who maintains it in year fifteen.

Ask on our projectWho waters the planting, who pays for it, and does the public reach the green at all?

Why this is worth using rather than skipping. It forces the water conversation to the front instead of leaving it to a late-stage MEP note. In Riyadh water is the constraint that decides how much greenery is honest. Naming that early, in a structured way, is what separates a real ecological proposal from a rendered one.

SourcesSaving the Planet by Design, Routledge, 2019, chapter 4, where he calls the four together a "quatrobrid" system.His own chapter names: nature-based, hydrological, technological, anthropocentric infrastructure.

06 · The Service Core

Put the lifts where the sun is worst.

His single most portable move, and the one he says he is best known for. Lifts, stairs, plant rooms and lavatories do not need a view or a stable temperature. Put them on the face taking the worst solar load, and they become a thick thermal shield in front of the space people actually occupy. A large part of the cooling problem is then solved by planning, before any machinery is specified.

Take this one into Riyadh. It works harder for us than it did for him. At our latitude the punishing loads are low morning and afternoon sun striking east and west façades, which a horizontal louvre cannot stop and a deep occupied buffer can. It also produces the long north-south plan a desert tower wants anyway. Three buildings, one move, adjusted each time to the local worst case.

07 · Buildings That Make You Well

Not "does no harm". Actively makes you better.

Salutogenic design is the idea that a building should measurably improve the health of the people in it, rather than merely avoid making them ill. Most green building standards aim at the second. This aims at the first, and it is the strand of Yeang's work closest to our Holistic requirement.

The two halves fit together: ecology supplies clean air, clean water, food and contact with nature. Health design supplies the human half — daylight, movement, quiet, and a building people can read and feel in control of.

Reading on a planted courtyardNational Library, Singapore

How it actually gets built

  • 01
    Daylight and a view out, from every occupied deskThe most evidenced single health factor in buildings. Drives floor-plate depth, so it is a massing decision, not a fit-out one. Decide it now.
  • 02
    Design the movement in, don't leave it to a gymGenerous, daylit, pleasant stairs placed before the lifts. Walkable routes through the podium. Sky courts people cross rather than look at.
  • 03
    Air people can verifyFiltration sized for dust, not just for code, and air quality displayed publicly in lobbies. In Riyadh this is a genuine amenity and a sales argument.
  • 04
    Contact with nature at eye levelPlanting you can touch, smell and sit in, not planting you look at from across an atrium. It has to be within reach to do the work.
  • 05
    Let the temperature vary, on purposeA gradient from outside to core beats a hard step from 44 °C into 22 °C. People acclimatise, feel more comfortable, and the building spends less doing it.
  • 06
    Quiet, and places to be aloneAcoustic control and retreat space are consistently underweighted in towers and consistently top the list in occupant surveys.

SourcesYeang & Dilani (eds), Ecological and Salutogenic Design for a Sustainable Healthy Global Society, Cambridge Scholars, 2021.The term comes from Aaron Antonovsky's work on what generates health rather than what causes disease.Honest note: health entered Yeang's framework late, in 2021, through this collaboration. It is where he arrived, not where he started.

Take into chapter two

Three things to hold on to.

Start free

Shape, orientation and the service core before any machinery. If we cannot say which rung of the ladder each space sits on, we have not designed it yet.

Water decides the greenery

How much planting is honest on this site is a water question, not a rendering question. Answer it early and out loud.

Every green surface needs a job

Shade, habitat, cooling, food. Anything that is only there to look green will be the first thing value-engineered out.
