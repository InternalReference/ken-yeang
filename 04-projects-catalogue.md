## 3. Yeang's device vocabulary — the recurring kit of parts

Across four decades the same devices recur. This is the transferable content of his work, and the part most relevant to a new tower:

1. **Service core displaced to the hot façade.** The lift/stair/WC core is pushed to the east and/or west face so that the solid, unoccupied, thermally-tolerant part of the plan absorbs the worst solar gain and shields the occupied floor. Cores are **naturally ventilated and daylit** — no mechanical pressurisation, and a lift lobby with a view and a window. This is his single most portable idea.
2. **Sky courts / recessed terraces**, often **spiralling** up the façade — usable outdoor rooms in the sky, doubling as shading to the floor below, as ventilation inlets, and as planting beds.
3. **Continuous vertical landscaping** — planted ramps that run unbroken from ground to roof so that soil, and therefore ecological continuity, is never severed by the building. Tested at 1.5 km length at Solaris.
4. **Orientation-differentiated shading.** Not one façade but four: thin fins north/south, deep louvres or solid walls east/west, calibrated by solar-path analysis. Louvres frequently double as **light shelves**.
5. **Wind wing-walls and wind scoops** — vertical fins that catch and funnel prevailing wind into the plan for cross-ventilation (Menara UMNO is the test case).
6. **Naturally ventilated atria and "eco-cells"** — voids that bring daylight and air down into deep or basement space, including car parks.
7. **Rainwater capture and reuse**, typically harvested off the roof gardens and ramps to basement storage.
8. **PV-ready roof structures** — the "sun roof" that shades the top floor and is framed to receive panels later.
9. **The vertical linear park / ecological continuity** — the theory that a tall building must not break the horizontal ecological corridor but continue it vertically.

*The canonical bioclimatic skyscraper. If only one project is shown, it is this one.*

**Ecological devices** (mapped to the vocabulary in §3):

- **(1) Core on the hot façade** — the lift and stair core sits on the **east** face, shading the office floor from morning sun and acting as a heat sink. The core is **naturally ventilated and daylit**, not mechanically pressurised. *This is the clearest built demonstration of his core-placement theory anywhere in his work.*
- **(2) Spiralling sky courts** — recessed, landscaped terraces spiral up the cylindrical façade, serving simultaneously as ventilation inlets, shading to the floor beneath, planting beds and usable outdoor rooms.
- **(4) Orientation-differentiated shading** — aluminium louvres change by face: thin strips north/south, **wide bands covering most of the west glazing** against afternoon sun. The curvilinear roof overhang shades the south façade from high-angled sun.
- **(8) PV-ready sun roof** — a tubular steel crown shades the rooftop pool and recreation deck and was framed to **receive photovoltaic panels later**. *The panels were part of the design intent, not the delivered scope.*
- **(3) Planted base** — landscaped berms spiral from the ground up into the building, tying it to grade.
- Structure: **eight columns outside the building envelope**, freeing the interior plan.
- Planning inversion: private cellular offices placed in the **centre** of the plan, open-plan workspace at the **perimeter** near daylight — a reversal of then-standard practice.

**What it was testing:** that a tall corporate tower in the humid tropics could be climate-shaped rather than a sealed glass box — and that the bioclimatic devices could read as *architecture*, not as bolt-on green kit. It is the project that made "bioclimatic skyscraper" a recognised type.

**Performance data:** **None found — no measured or simulated energy figures could be located.** The Aga Khan technical documentation and the widely-cited academic essay on the building both describe **design intent only**. Any percentage saving quoted for Mesiniaga elsewhere should be treated as unsourced. **[NO PERFORMANCE DATA]**

**Known in-use problems** (useful for a credible, non-naive presentation): reported **rust and corrosion on metal finishes**, and **water pooling in the sky courts** in tropical rain. Source: ArchDaily AD Classics. This is the honest counterpoint to the vertical-planting idea and is worth acknowledging rather than hiding.

**Awards:** **Aga Khan Award for Architecture, 1995** (1993–95 cycle). Jury citation: the building represents "a successful and promising approach to the design of many-storied structures in a tropical climate." Juror Charles Jencks described it as "a striking alternative to the reigning mode of corporate towers."

**Ecological devices:**

- **(5) Wind wing-walls — the headline device.** Projecting vertical fins on the façade catch prevailing wind and funnel it into the plan, creating positive pressure at inlets and pulling air across each floor. The building is described as the **first office tower in Malaysia designed for natural ventilation at height** via this mechanism.
- **Operable windows on every floor**, controlled by users — combined with the wing walls this allows floors to run naturally ventilated in favourable conditions rather than being permanently sealed.
- **(2) Individual balconies to each floor** — every floor gets its own external terrace, both as amenity and as part of the ventilation path.
- **(4) Variable shading** — shading devices differ by orientation rather than being uniform.
- **(1) Core placement** used, as elsewhere, as a solar buffer on the tight site.

**What it was testing:** whether a **high-rise office floor can be naturally ventilated** — the hardest claim in tropical tall-building design, because wind speed and pressure at height normally force sealed façades. The wing wall is his proposed answer: shape the façade to capture and direct wind rather than resist it. **This is the device with the most direct read-across to a hot-arid tower**, where the equivalent problem is capturing cool night air and shaded breeze while excluding dust and heat.

**Performance data:** **None found.** No measured ventilation rates, hours of natural-ventilation operation, or energy figures could be located. Claims that the building "runs naturally ventilated" are **architect/design-stage descriptions**, not metered results. **[NO MEASURED DATA — CLAIMED ONLY]**

**Awards:** Widely published and frequently cited as a landmark of bioclimatic design. **No specific award with a confirmed name, year and awarding body could be verified.** **[UNVERIFIED]** — do not attribute an award to this building without checking.

**Images / drawings:** the wing-wall **section diagrams with airflow arrows** are the single most useful drawing in Yeang's entire output for explaining the idea. Look for them in his books (*The Skyscraper Bioclimatically Considered*; *The Green Skyscraper*) and:

**Ecological devices:**

- **(3) & (9) The 1.5 km "Vertical Linear Park"** — a **continuous ecological ramp** spiralling around the building from ground level to roof, unbroken, planted along its whole length. This is the device the project exists to prove: that landscape can run **continuously** from grade to roof so the building extends the ground-plane ecology vertically rather than severing it. **The 1.5 km length is a design/measured-geometry figure from the architect, not an ecological outcome.**

**What it was testing:** the **green plot ratio** proposition — that a building can return *more* planted area to the site than the footprint it took away, by wrapping landscape around and up the form. It is the built, funded, delivered version of what EDITT Tower proposed on paper.

> **Direct warning on this project.** EDITT is the single most over-quoted scheme in Yeang's output. Very specific numbers circulate widely — a storey count, a GFA, and headline percentages for **water self-sufficiency, PV-generated energy share, and "organic mass" / planting ratio**. **None of these could be verified from a primary source in this research** (the dedicated Wikipedia article does not exist; the usual aggregator pages returned 403/404).
>
> Every one of those percentages, even if correctly transcribed, would be a **[DESIGN CLAIM]** for an **unbuilt** competition entry — a target for a building that was never constructed, never commissioned and never metered. **Do not put an EDITT performance percentage on a slide.** Use EDITT for its *idea*, and show the drawing.

**The idea it was testing** (this is well-attested and is what makes it useful):

- **(3) & (9) Continuous vegetated ramp** running from the street up through the whole tower, so that public realm and planting climb the building instead of stopping at the lobby — the tower as a *continuation of the ground*, not an interruption of it.
- **(7) Closed-loop water** — rainwater and greywater captured, treated and reused on site.
- **Designed for disassembly** — components detailed to be taken apart and reused rather than demolished. This is an unusually early statement of circular-economy thinking in a tall building and is arguably the most contemporary-feeling part of the scheme.
- **On-site energy generation** via photovoltaics integrated into the envelope.
- **Continuous ecological corridor** — the theoretical claim that a tower can carry biodiversity vertically.

**Why it still matters:** EDITT is the clearest single image of Yeang's whole argument, and because it is unbuilt it is *drawing-led* — which is exactly what a presentation wants. Its value is rhetorical and diagrammatic, not evidential.

**Awards:** 2nd prize in the competition [VERIFIED]. No built-work awards (it was never built).

**Images / drawings:** the **sectional perspective showing the planted ramp spiralling the full height** is the iconic image. Best found in his books — *The Green Skyscraper* and *Reinventing the Skyscraper* — and in image searches under "EDITT Tower Yeang section". **Verify licensing before use;** no open-licensed primary source was located.

### 4.1 1980s — the first bioclimatic experiments

#### Roof-Roof House — Ampang, Selangor / Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia (1984)
*His own house. The origin of everything — the "environmental filter" idea, tested at 1:1 on himself.*

| Field | Value | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Ampang, Selangor (Greater Kuala Lumpur), Malaysia | [VERIFIED] |
| Client | **Ken Yeang himself** — his own residence | [VERIFIED] |
| Architect | T.R. Hamzah & Yeang (Ken Yeang) | [VERIFIED] |
| Year | **1984** per the Merdeka Award citation and The Star; **1985** per Wikipedia and the practice's own site. **Sources conflict — 1984 is the better-attested design/completion date.** | [VERIFIED with conflict] |
| Status | **Built** | [VERIFIED] |
| Floors / area | Not established | **[UNVERIFIED]** |
| Programme | Private house | [VERIFIED] |

**Ecological devices:**

- **The double roof — the "environmental filter".** A **lower roof terrace acts as the primary roof**, and a **raised louvred roof sits above it as a secondary layer**. The upper "umbrella" roof **blocks solar radiation while letting hot air escape between the louvres**, so the trapped air layer is continuously purged instead of becoming a heat store. The practice describes the result as a **"house within a house"** that lets the building **"breathe naturally"** without mechanical plant.
- **Wide overhangs** and **large openings** to drive cross-ventilation.
- **Open-plan layout** so air moves through the whole volume rather than being compartmented.
- A **swimming pool positioned on the windward side** so incoming air is cooled by evaporation before entering the house is a frequently-repeated description of this house. **[UNVERIFIED — could not confirm from a primary source in this research]**

**What it was testing:** the founding proposition of his entire career — that **the building envelope is a climatic filter, not a barrier**. Everything later (the sun roof at Mesiniaga, the wing walls at UMNO, the shading vocabulary) is this idea scaled up and moved onto a tower. The Merdeka Award citation explicitly names this house as the early demonstration of the principles that led to the bioclimatic skyscraper.

**Performance data:** **None. [NO MEASURED DATA]** No temperature, comfort or energy monitoring was found.

**Awards:** none verified for the house itself. Its significance is historiographic — it is cited in the **2011 Merdeka Award** citation as the origin point of his method.

**Images / drawings:** the **sectional diagram of the two roofs with airflow arrows** is one of the most reproduced drawings in tropical architecture and is ideal for a presentation.

**Why the climate matters:** Manesar/Gurgaon sits in a **hot semi-arid (Köppen BSh)** zone — long very hot dry summers, large diurnal swing, dust, and a short monsoon. It is **not** the humid tropics of Kuala Lumpur and Singapore where the rest of his built work sits. **This is the only significant Yeang tower in a genuinely hot-dry climate**, which makes it the single most transferable precedent for Riyadh.

**Ecological devices:**

- **(3) Spiral landscaped ramps on BOTH north and south façades** — continuous **green planter ramps with a pedestrian walkway**, plus **vegetated green walls** and **a series of sky terraces**, climbing the full height. The north and south systems mirror each other so the planting wraps the tower.
- **(6) Eco-cells at ground level, north and south** — voids that carry **vegetation, daylight and natural ventilation down into the basement car-park levels**. A direct answer to the "how do you green a podium/parking base" problem.
- **(4) Sun-shading louvres set from solar-path analysis, doubling as light shelves** — orientation-specific, not uniform.
- **(7) Self-sufficient water reuse/recycling loop** — rainwater collected from the **roof gardens and the perimeter ramps**, channelled to **basement storage** for internal reuse. In a water-scarce hot-dry site this is the device with the highest strategic value.
- **Naturally ventilated toilets and lift lobbies** — the low-cost, high-yield version of his naturally-ventilated-core idea.

**What it was testing:** whether the wet-tropics vertical-landscape vocabulary **survives transplantation into a hot, dry, dusty climate** — where planting needs irrigation it cannot get for free, and where the shading problem dominates the ventilation problem. **Whether it actually worked is unestablished**, and that is itself the most useful finding: it is an open question a Riyadh proposal can position itself as answering.

## THE MODE HIERARCHY, stated in his own words on the panel

This is the clearest published statement of the passive/mixed/full mode system anywhere in the research:

- **Passive Mode** — built-form orientation, daylight optimisation, solar orientation, sunshading, light shelves, natural ventilation, responsive façade design, building colours, extensive skycourt landscaping.
- **Mixed Mode** — at transitional spaces (lobbies, foyers), where natural ventilation is *"supplemented by mechanical means such as fans."*
- **Full Mode** — full air-conditioning and artificial light for the library workspaces and collection areas, *"for the protection of book collections and is an acknowledgement that comfort is as much a question of lifestyle and perception as it is about cost and energy."*

**That last clause is important and unusually candid.** He concedes that comfort expectation, not physics, sets the limit on how far passive design can go. For a Riyadh client who expects full conditioning, that is a far more credible starting position than a purist passive argument.

## The service core as thermal buffer — a THIRD building

Verbatim from the panel: *"The elevator service-cores serve as thermal buffers to the North-West and South-West facades."*

Mesiniaga (east), Menara UMNO (east), National Library (north-west and south-west). **Three buildings, same move, orientation adjusted to each site's worst solar exposure.** This is now firmly established as his signature and it is the strongest single transfer to Riyadh.

## The best teaching diagram in the entire asset set

Panel 2 carries a diagram titled **"Composite of Thermal Buffers"**, showing in exploded form: south-west screen, south-east screen, the Main Library block, *elevator core and toilets as north-east buffer*, *north-east block as solar buffer*, and *last floor as roof solar buffer*. Alongside it: *Passive-Mode by Building Orientation*, *Passive-Mode by Cross Ventilation*, and an *SW–SE Section*.

If the team is to be taught one idea, this diagram teaches it.

The same panel carries all 16 floor plans, three elevations, and an annotated cutaway naming: naturally ventilated atrium, "The Great Wall" of books, **sunshading with 9-metre-deep blades**, sky terraces landscaped to enhance site biodiversity, **"Eco-Cells" bringing daylight and natural ventilation down to B1**, the naturally ventilated "street", and **3-metre-high trees planted at the sky courts**.

## MAJOR FINDING — he claims embodied energy analysis, in 2004

The panel's "Other Innovative Applications" list includes:

> **Building Energy Embodiment Analysis** · Green Materials & Specification · Buildability & Construction Efficiency · **Design for Disassembly** · Design for Adaptive Reuse · Acoustics Considerations · Design for Maintenance · Coherent M/E Systems · Intelligent-Building Systems · Water Conservation Systems

**Embodied energy analysis and design for disassembly are claimed as applied features on a 2004 building.** That is roughly fifteen years before embodied carbon became mainstream discourse.

**This sharpens the carbon critique for a third and final time.** The defensible claim is now very narrow, and correspondingly very strong:

> He was analysing embodied energy in 2004 and says so on the record. **What has never been published, for any of his buildings, is a number** — not for embodied energy, not for whole-life carbon, and not for measured operational performance.

The framework and the intent were there decades early. The evidence never followed. That is a far more interesting story for the team than "he ignored carbon", and it is true.
